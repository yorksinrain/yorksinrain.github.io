01 — HARDWARE · FABRICATION

Muto-box

A modular electronics panel that hangs on the back of a performer's powered wheelchair, carrying the compute, motor-control and audio units of a wheelchair-based robot-arm performance system. Every module sits in its own 3D-printed tray, and every port stays reachable so a cable can be re-plugged in seconds during a live show.

Time
Sep.–Nov. 2026
Type
Team project · Embodied Media Lab
Area
Hardware design // Digital fabrication
Tools
3D modeling, Bambu Lab H2S, laser cutter
v1.0 finalized · Nov. 2026
MY ROLE
I did
Measured every electronic unit, planned the layout and cable routing, modeled the board and all 7 module trays, fabricated them (laser cutting and 3D printing), and assembled and tested the panel on the wheelchair.
Team
The lab team set the requirements (e.g. fast re-plugging during shows) and reviewed each version. The robot arm, software and other parts of the system were built by other members.

ITERATIONS

  1. Measure & modelSep. 2026Measured each unit, planned routing and placement, then printed the first Pi mount and USB extender housing and test-fitted them with real hardware.
  2. Cardboard prototypeOct. 2026400 × 292 mm, two glued 5 mm cardboard layers, laser-cut with a 20 mm slot grid to check the layout at full size.
  3. 3D-printed boardLate Oct. 2026390 × 330 × 5 mm in two pieces (the printer bed was too small for one), joined by a V tongue-and-groove finger joint, 4 × M2 screws and glue. All 7 trays printed with 4 rounded hooks each; the hook test passed.
  4. Panel 2.0 → v1.0Nov. 2026Reprinted the board with front-side port openings, then assembled and tested it on the wheelchair with everything plugged in.

DESIGN DECISIONS

  • Re-pluggable under pressure. Something always comes loose during a show, so each port gets free space in front and an opening beside it. All other cable length is coiled on the back.
  • A layout without clashes. Both U2D2 controllers sit on the right edge so their thick cables wrap to the back. The amplifier sits below the Pi, offset so its DC and RCA plugs don't hit the Pi's USB-C.
  • Swappable modules. Pegboard-style hooks let each tray be lifted off and replaced without tools: Raspberry Pi, 2 × U2D2, piezo sensor switch, USB extender, headphone amplifier and power strip.

02 — RESEARCH · BRANDING · PITCH

Enlight

An AI browser extension that detects deceptive interface patterns on web pages and explains them to users in plain language. Its tagline: “Bring Dark Patterns Into the Light.” The extension's character, Cross Face, is a black sphere whose only feature is a cross drawn on its surface. Turning, nodding and tilting the cross is enough to show every state, so it needs no eyes or mouth.

Time
Oct. 2026
Type
Team project · Build With Claude Hackathon
Area
User research // Brand // Interaction states
Tool
Figma
MY ROLE
I did
User research and the survey, brand identity, and the pitch.
Team
The extension and its AI detection were coded by a teammate.

Generative AI: Enlight's detection and explanation features are built on Claude (Anthropic) for the Build With Claude Hackathon.

FACE = A CROSS ON A SPHERE

  • Turn left / right: the vertical line bends.
  • Nod / look up: the horizontal line bends into ∪ / ∩.
  • Tilt: the whole cross rotates.
  • Line weight, length and dashes: how strong a state is.

12 STATES, ONE COMPONENT

  • Main flow: Idle → Inspect → Thinking → Notice → Explain → Repair → Compare → Done.
  • Branches: Undo, Unsure (explains only, never edits), Paused and Hidden.
  • All 12 states are variants of one Figma component, so every screen uses the same face.
